## Report 2 — Macroeconomic Stability, Monetary Policy Tightening, Inflation Management, Fiscal Consolidation, External Sector Vulnerability, Currency Pressure, Growth Resilience
**Source**: `20261027_1704.pdf`  |  **Date**: 2026-10-27
**Themes**: Macroeconomic Stability, Monetary Policy Tightening, Inflation Management, Fiscal Consolidation, External Sector Vulnerability, Currency Pressure, Growth Resilience
**Time horizon**: 6-12M  |  **Conviction**: High

> Deutsche Bank's India special report projects resilient 7%+ real GDP growth in FY27 and FY28 despite multiple headwinds including higher oil prices, sub-normal monsoon, and geopolitical uncertainty. However, elevated inflation averaging 6.0% in Oct-Dec'26 with core pressures strengthening and household inflation expectations rising to 10% necessitate 100bp of cumulative RBI tightening to safeguard both price and financial stability, with repo rate expected to reach 6.25% by 1H'27. While fiscal consolidation appears manageable for FY27 through RBI dividend support, FY28 faces significant challenges from persistent oil prices, subsidy requirements, and 8th Pay Commission implementation, while external sector vulnerabilities including rupee weakening beyond 97/USD and declining FX reserves pose near-term risks.

### Key points
- FY27 and FY28 real GDP growth expected above 7% despite external headwinds and sub-normal monsoon
- CPI inflation forecast at 6.0% in Oct-Dec'26, 5.0% in FY27, 4.7% in FY28 with upside risks from fuel price increases
- RBI expected to tighten by 100bp cumulatively: 25bp in Dec'26 and 50bp in 1H'27, taking repo to 6.25%
- Durable liquidity surplus could fall to INR 2trn or less by end-FY27 from INR 10trn at end-September
- Central government likely to meet FY27 fiscal deficit target of 4.3% of GDP supported by RBI dividend of INR 2.9trn
- Rupee expected to weaken beyond 97/USD during 2027; FX reserves declined USD 60bn from early-September peak of USD 786bn
- Core and core ex-precious metals inflation likely to converge around 4.3-4.5% by Jan-Mar'27; WPI inflation above 11% signals cost pass-through
- Household inflation expectations risen to 10% from 8% pre-war, reinforcing risk of second-round effects
- Manufacturing PMI at 52.8 in Aug'26; Services PMI at 54.1; both moderating but above 50 indicating expansion
- Credit growth accelerating at 21.0% yoy in Sep'26; M3 broad money supply at 19.1% yoy

**Catalysts**: December 2026 RBI MPC decision on repo rate, 1H'27 monetary policy tightening cycle continuation, FY27 fiscal deficit achievement and RBI dividend realization, Monsoon performance and agricultural sector vulnerability, Oil price movements and domestic fuel price adjustments, Global macro backdrop improvement for rupee support, 8th Pay Commission implementation in FY28, FII flow dynamics and external stability, Core inflation convergence trajectory, Household inflation expectations anchoring
