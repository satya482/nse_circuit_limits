> India's shipbuilding sector is entering a multi-decade structural growth phase driven by a critical imbalance between its $1.28 trillion maritime trade value (70% sea-dependent) and less than 1% global shipbuilding capacity. Backed by Rs.69,725 crore government policy support, aggressive capacity expansion by Cochin Shipyard, Mazagon Dock, and GRSE, and a favourable global shipbuilding upcycle ($166Bn growing to $274Bn by 2034E), domestic yards are positioned to capture a portion of the $8.1 billion Indian market opportunity by FY33. ### Key points
- India carries only 5% of its own EXIM cargo on Indian-flagged vessels despite 70% trade dependency on sea routes, creating structural opportunity
- Global shipbuilding market expanding from $166.2Bn (2025) to $274.2Bn (2034E) at 5.72% CAGR driven by IMO regulations and energy transition
- Indian commercial shipbuilding market projected to grow 25% CAGR from $1.1Bn (2024) to $8.1Bn (FY33)
- Rs.69,725 crore government policy framework provides sustained financial support through Shipbuilding Development Scheme and Maritime Development Fund
- India's three major yards (Cochin, Mazagon, GRSE) executing Rs.30,000+ crore capacity expansion with new dry docks, block fabrication facilities, and geographic diversification
- China, South Korea, Japan control 97% of global shipbuilding; India's <1% share presents white space opportunity as order books reach 191mn CGT (highest since 2011)
- Defence indigenisation provides additional structural demand with India ranking 6th in global naval fleet size but critically underdeveloped domestic capacity

**Catalysts**: Commissioning of new dry docks and block fabrication facilities across major yards, Government order book conversion from defence contracts to commercial shipbuilding, IMO CII/EEXI regulations forcing fleet replacement cycle acceleration, Maritime India Vision 2047 target of 5% global shipbuilding share by 2047, Domestic supplier ecosystem development reducing import dependence, LNG and ammonia carrier demand from global energy transition, Public-private collaboration framework materialisation
