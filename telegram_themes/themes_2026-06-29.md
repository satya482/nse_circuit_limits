### Key points
- Global interest rate regime has structurally shifted; ultra-low rates of 2009-2021 are unlikely to return due to sovereign debt, fiscal deficits, de-globalization and geopolitical fragmentation
- Traditional 60/40 portfolio correlation has reverted to 1980s-style positive correlation, reducing diversification benefits in inflationary environment
- India's REIT and InvIT market scaled to INR 10 Tn AUM and INR 5 Tn market cap in 9 years; projected to reach INR 20 Tn AUM by 2030
- Real assets with contractual, inflation-linked cash flows provide superior inflation hedging and portfolio resilience compared to traditional bonds
- Global precedent from USA, Australia, Japan demonstrates REITs/InvITs can scale substantially through institutional participation, regulatory support and product innovation
- India's domestic capital pools (EPFO, NPS, insurance, pension funds, foreign investors) represent INR 11.6 Tn additional investment potential by 2030
- Annual primary market opportunity for REITs and InvITs could exceed INR 1 Tn, enabling significant capital formation

**Catalysts**: EPFO and NPS integration into REIT and InvIT investments, Introduction of REIT and InvIT-focused ETFs and passive products, Regulatory expansion into new real asset classes (data centres, logistics, digital infrastructure), Deeper institutional participation and market consolidation, Development of benchmark indices and inclusion in broader market indices
