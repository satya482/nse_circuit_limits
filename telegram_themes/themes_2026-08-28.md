**Source**: `msg_1595`  |  **Date**: 2026-08-27
**Themes**: US-China Trade Dynamics, Inflation and Disinflation Cycles, Supply Chain Fragmentation, Interest Rate Normalization, Geopolitical Competition, Capital Market Restructuring
**Time horizon**: 2Y+  |  **Conviction**: High

> The decades-long US-China economic relationship that exported disinflation through cheap manufacturing and recycled capital through Treasury purchases is structurally breaking. As supply chains fragment, tariffs rise, and geopolitical competition intensifies, the US faces a new regime of higher inflation and structurally elevated interest rates with uncertain Treasury demand dynamics.

### Key points
- China's three-decade export-disinflation model is structurally breaking down as wages rise and geopolitical tensions escalate
- US Treasury demand from Chinese reserve recycling may become less predictable, potentially pushing bond yields higher
- Domestic manufacturing reshoring and tariff-driven supply chain fragmentation will likely increase goods inflation versus the deflationary import regime
- Structural interest rate normalization ahead as foreign demand for US government debt becomes strategically uncertain
- The integrated goods-capital system that defined globalization is fragmenting into strategic regional blocs

**Catalysts**: Tariff implementation and trade policy escalation, Chinese foreign exchange reserve deployment shifts, US Treasury auction dynamics and yield curve movement, Supply chain reshoring announcements, Geopolitical flashpoints affecting capital flows
