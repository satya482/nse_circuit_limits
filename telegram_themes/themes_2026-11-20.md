## Report 1 — Solar Manufacturing Integration, Domestic Cell Supply Scarcity, DCR Premium Dynamics, Technology Transition to TOPCon, US Export Market Closure, Grid Constraints and Demand Segmentation, Capex Intensity and Balance Sheet Strength
**Source**: `20261120_1715.pdf`  |  **Date**: 2026-11-20
**Themes**: Solar Manufacturing Integration, Domestic Cell Supply Scarcity, DCR Premium Dynamics, Technology Transition to TOPCon, US Export Market Closure, Grid Constraints and Demand Segmentation, Capex Intensity and Balance Sheet Strength
**Time horizon**: 6-12M  |  **Conviction**: High

> India's solar manufacturing sector is shifting from module-centric capacity additions to cell-integrated value chains, with integration now defining profitability. Cell-integrated players earn 31% EBITDA margins versus 7.7% for module-only manufacturers, driven by severe domestic cell scarcity (57GW cells vs 205GW modules) and 70% DCR premiums. However, a 124GW cell capacity wave arriving in FY27-28 against 60GW demand will compress these spreads by FY28, making execution, balance-sheet strength and captive cell ownership critical differentiators. Premier Energies is the top pick for superior cell integration, while module-only players and leveraged peers face margin compression and execution risk.

### Key points
- Cell-integrated manufacturers earn ~31% EBITDA margins vs 7.7-17.6% for module-only peers, a 23+ percentage point gap
- Domestic cell capacity shortage (57.1GW cells vs 204.9GW modules) supports DCR premiums of Rs8.9/Wp (~69%) over non-DCR
- 124GW cell capacity supply wave expected by FY27E against 60GW demand will reset DCR premiums downward in FY28
- US AD/CVD duties of 249.13% effective Oct-2026 close Indian export market; Waaree wrote off 3GW+ orders
- Cell line utilization at 81-99% vs module lines at 44-68% reflects structural imbalance favoring integrated players
- Module-only manufacturer margins have fallen 23 percentage points over five quarters from Q1FY26 to Q2FY27
- DCR spreads of USD7-8 cents/Wp vs non-DCR USD1-2 cents/Wp create unsustainable economics for non-integrated peers
- 150GW+ RE construction pipeline supports volumes through FY28, but new award flow weakness threatens FY29-30 demand
- Waaree maintains net cash of Rs32.2bn despite Rs43.3bn H1 capex; weaker peers face debt-funded expansion at peak capex cycle
