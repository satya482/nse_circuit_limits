## Report 4 — De-Dollarization & Reserve Diversification, Gold as Reserve Asset, Currency Competition, Central Bank Policy
**Source**: `msg_1647`  |  **Date**: 2026-09-17
**Themes**: De-Dollarization & Reserve Diversification, Gold as Reserve Asset, Currency Competition, Central Bank Policy
**Time horizon**: 2Y+  |  **Conviction**: High

> While de-dollarization headlines dominate, the US Dollar's structural dominance in global reserves (~57%) remains unshaken due to absence of credible alternatives. Central banks are strategically diversifying into gold—now a major reserve asset—purchasing ~30% of global annual production, representing a structural multi-year tailwind for precious metals. The real transition is reserves shifting away from dollars, not trade shifting away from dollar infrastructure.

### Key points
- US Dollar remains dominant at 57% of global FX reserves despite de-dollarization narratives
- Central banks purchasing ~30% of annual global gold production, making them major demand drivers
- Gold slowing in 2026 due to 60% price rise year-on-year, but reserve diversification structural trend continues

- Liquidity-constrained nations like Turkey and Russia selling gold to meet FX/USD needs despite reserve diversification intent

**Catalysts**: Central bank reserve rebalancing cycles, Gold price stabilization post-60% rally, RMB convertibility & capital market reforms, US fiscal policy & dollar strength volatility, Geopolitical events increasing reserve currency mistrust
