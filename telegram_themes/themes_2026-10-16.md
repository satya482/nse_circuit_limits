## Report 1 — FII Capital Flows, India Valuation Premium, Emerging Markets Competition, AI Trade Rotation, Currency Risk, US Treasury Competition
**Source**: `msg_1699`  |  **Date**: 2026-10-16
**Themes**: FII Capital Flows, India Valuation Premium, Emerging Markets Competition, AI Trade Rotation, Currency Risk, US Treasury Competition
**Time horizon**: 6-12M  |  **Conviction**: Medium

> FII capital remains elusive for India despite domestic resilience, as investors weigh expensive valuations, lack of direct AI exposure, and attractive US dollar returns. India's 20-25x PE premium to emerging markets, combined with moderate earnings growth and currency headwinds, makes it uncompetitive against cheaper AI and semiconductor plays in Taiwan, Korea, and China. Without significant valuation correction or a new structural catalyst, incremental FII flows remain unlikely despite domestic SIP and insurance support cushioning markets.

### Key points
- India trades at 20-25x PE versus China at 10-12x PE, making it relatively expensive for FIIs
- India lacks direct large-cap AI beneficiaries compared to US tech, Taiwan semiconductors, and Korean memory chips
- FIIs evaluate returns in USD; rupee depreciation erodes returns despite rupee-denominated gains
- US Treasury yields of 4-5% provide risk-free alternative to EM equity risk
- India's earnings growth moderation cannot justify premium valuations
- Korea, Taiwan, and China offer better AI and semiconductor exposure at lower valuations
- India no longer under-owned; most EM allocators have meaningful India exposure
- Domestic SIP and insurance flows cushion Indian markets, preventing valuation corrections that would attract FIIs

**Catalysts**: Significant earnings acceleration in Indian corporates, Meaningful valuation correction to EM-comparable multiples, Emergence of large-cap Indian AI or semiconductor champions, US Treasury yield compression, Rupee stabilization against USD, New macroeconomic catalyst for India-specific rotation
