Context

Three tailwinds, one pipe company

USA — AI & LNG Supercycle

The AI data-centre boom has created an unexpected new customer for steel pipes. Each mega data centre needs a dedicated gas-fired power plant, and each power plant needs pipelines. Kinder Morgan estimates US natural gas demand will hit 150 Bcf per day by 2031. The INGAA Foundation forecasts a $1 trillion pipeline buildout over 26 years — 40,000 new miles of transmission capacity. US LNG exports are expected to reach 17 Bcf/d in 2026 and nearly double to over 30 Bcf/d by the early 2030s. This is the strongest sustained pipe demand cycle in a generation.

Saudi Arabia — Vision 2030 Rearmament

Saudi Aramco has raised its capex guidance to US$52–55 billion and plans to grow gas production from 11.8 Bcf/day to 16.6 Bcf/day by 2030 — requiring 4,000 km of new gas pipelines. Reconstruction spending across Syria and neighbouring countries adds further demand. Two-thirds of the Saudi DI pipe market currently relies on imports. Welspun is building localised manufacturing in the Kingdom for both line pipes and ductile iron pipes, positioning itself as an import-substitution play.

India — JJM Revival + Domestic O&G Awakening

After two soft quarters, India's water infrastructure cycle is turning. The Union Budget allocated ₹70,000 crores to Jal Jeevan Mission, which has been extended to 2028. River-interlinking mega-projects (Ken-Betwa, PKC, ERCP, Marathwada Grid) represent 2–3 million tonnes of DI pipe demand in 3–5 years. Simultaneously, India's CGD network will see ₹40,000 crore in investment until 2034, and GAIL is investing ₹2,000–3,000 crore in FY26 for pipeline infrastructure. Water infrastructure in India is on track to grow at 11.6% CAGR to reach US$17.9 billion by FY29.

Business Model

Making the arteries that move energy and water

Welspun Corp makes large-diameter steel pipes — the physical infrastructure that moves oil, gas, and water from where it is to where it's needed. The company operates five business lines spread across three continents: India, the United States, and Saudi Arabia.

The Flywheel

Welspun wins project orders by offering end-to-end supply — pipes, corrosion coatings, concrete coating, bends, and bend coatings — along with technical certifications for hydrogen-grade and deep-sea applications that few competitors hold. Getting certified for a new market (Argentina, Australia, Europe) permanently expands the addressable pool. Each project executed becomes a reference for the next. Over 8 of the last 10 years, Welspun has broken the 1-million-tonne-per-year global line pipe sales mark — an extraordinary consistency in a project-driven business.

Vertical Stories

Five engines, three geographies

USA — Line Pipes
Little Rock: Booked solid until FY28

Welspun's mill in Little Rock, Arkansas currently runs a spiral mill that is fully booked through March 2028. A new LSAW (longitudinal submerged arc welded) mill is coming online in Q4 FY26 and a new HFIW mill in Q1 FY27. These additions will materially expand US volumes. The external demand driver is concrete: midstream operators are building 8–9 major new pipelines to feed LNG export terminals and data-centre power plants. The Strait of Hormuz conflict has added further urgency to US-based pipeline supply chains.

Status: ✦ Running & ramping | New capacity H2 FY26

Saudi Arabia — Line Pipes + DI Pipes
Two greenfield plants in the Kingdom

Welspun is building a new LSAW pipe facility in Saudi Arabia — with the dual logic of serving Saudi Aramco's domestic 4,000 km pipeline programme and acting as an export hub for markets currently unreachable from India due to antidumping duties. Separately, a greenfield DI pipe plant targets the Saudi water infrastructure market, where 2/3rds of consumption is currently imported. The Saudi government is already investigating antidumping duties on cheap DI pipe imports — which would accelerate Welspun's import substitution story once production begins. Management expects Saudi projects to begin contributing from Q2 FY27 onwards.

Status: ✦✦ Under construction | First steel by Q2 FY27

India — DI Pipes (Ductile Iron)
JJM revival: ₹70,000 Cr budget, projects starting Q1 FY27

DI pipes serve the water infrastructure market — Jal Jeevan Mission, AMRUT 2, irrigation, and sewerage. After a two-quarter funding lull (government payments were delayed while an audit was completed), management confirms the audit is done and spending is expected from Q1 FY27. The mega river-linking projects alone could generate 2–3 million tonnes of pipe demand over 3–5 years. Welspun also began exporting DI pipes to Europe, Middle East, and Africa, with an export infrastructure now fully ready. Q3 FY26 volumes grew 39% YoY despite the domestic slowdown.

Status: ✦ Running | Domestic recovery expected Q1 FY27

India — Line Pipes (Export O&G)
India as an export hub for non-tariff global markets

India-origin pipe exports are going to Australia, Qatar, Latin America, and Europe — markets with no antidumping barriers. A first breakthrough in Argentina in Q3 FY26 was notable, won on quality and technical superiority. India also benefits from upcoming Free Trade Agreements (particularly with the EU) that will open new export corridors. For domestic O&G, GAIL, BPCL, and IOCL are expected to tender multiple large projects in FY27 as LNG capacity at Indian terminals grows from 52.7 MMTPA to 86.9 MMTPA.

Status: ✦ Running, FTA optionality building

Sintex — Water Tanks + OPVC Pipes
Brand revival + new category entry

Sintex (acquired from resolution proceedings) is India's most recognised water tank brand. Management is systematically rebuilding market share via dealer expansion (7,000 unique billed outlets, up 25% in H1 FY26), the Sintex Eterno 50-year warranty premium tank, and WPL women's cricket sponsorship. The bigger prize is OPVC pipes — a newer plastic pipe technology for water distribution with a very large total addressable market. Sintex has now cleared approvals and empanelment in Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and southern states. Execution of the OPVC order backlog has started.

Status: ✦ Tanks ramping | OPVC early innings

Strategic Insight

The one thing to understand

The Non-Obvious Insight

Welspun Corp is not an Indian pipe exporter. It is three local manufacturers — one American, one Saudi, one Indian — that happen to share an owner. When the US imposed antidumping duties on Indian line pipes, Welspun's response was not to lobby for exemptions. It was to build a factory in Arkansas. When Saudi Arabia restricts imports, Welspun's answer is a greenfield plant in the Kingdom. This "local everywhere" strategy means tariffs, trade wars, and import restrictions — events that hurt every Indian exporter — are events that help Welspun, by raising the bar against the Chinese and Korean competition while Welspun sits safely inside the tariff wall. The US mill is fully booked until FY28 not because of Indian pipe exports, but because American pipeline companies would rather buy from an American manufacturer.

Risks — Eyes Open

Five things that could go wrong

⚠️
Simultaneous capex on 7–8 projects
Welspun is executing new mills in the US (LSAW + HFIW), Saudi Arabia (DI + LSAW), and multiple India projects concurrently. Any delays push revenue recognition into later quarters and tie up working capital. Signal: watch for capex guidance slippage or delays in first-steel dates disclosed each quarter.
⚠️
India government spending unpredictability
JJM funds were slow in FY25–H1 FY26 despite budget allocations. The domestic DI pipe and water-sector pipe business is entirely dependent on state governments actually spending money. The FY26 budget gives comfort, but the same risk repeats every year. Signal: Monitor quarterly DI pipe volume — two consecutive soft quarters below 80 KMT would be a red flag.
⚠️
Hot-rolled coil price volatility
Line pipes are essentially fabricated HR coil. Sharp HRC price spikes compress margins, especially in fixed-price contracts. The pass-through lag can be 1–2 quarters. When input costs rise faster than realisations, margins compress. Signal: Watch gross margin and EBITDA per tonne in back-to-back quarters when HRC prices are rising sharply.
⚠️
US political / regulatory risk
The "local manufacturer" story depends on Welspun's US entity being treated as an American company for tariff purposes. Any change in Buy America provisions, tightening of domestic content rules, or aggressive trade investigation could challenge this positioning. Welspun's US subsidiary is operationally domestic but Indian-owned — a distinction that could matter under certain political scenarios. Magnitude: high but probability low given current administration's infrastructure push.
⚠️
Sintex integration drag on group margins
Sintex and TMT rebars are structurally lower-margin than large-diameter steel pipes. As these segments scale, they may dilute consolidated EBITDA margin even as headline revenues grow. The brand revival is real, but OPVC pipe market penetration in competitive state-level tender markets could take longer than management's timeline. Signal: Watch Sintex EBITDA contribution (or losses) disclosed quarterly.

Watch Signals

Five metrics, five minutes per concall

Global order book trajectory
Currently ₹25,350 Cr. Good: breaching ₹28,000 Cr signals continued demand strength and allows pricing power. Red flag: order book declining below ₹20,000 Cr would mean bookings aren't replacing executions.
US mill utilisation & first revenue from new LSAW
Watch Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 disclosures for when the new US LSAW mill achieves commercial production. A positive: first order execution from the new LSAW mill on schedule. Red flag: any communication of delay beyond Q1 FY27.
DI pipe volumes recovering post JJM revival
Q3 FY26 was 92 KMT. With JJM funds expected to flow from Q1 FY27, watch for DI pipe volumes consistently exceeding 100 KMT per quarter. That would confirm the government spending cycle has genuinely restarted. Anything below 75 KMT in Q2 FY27 would be a red flag.
Saudi first-steel date for LSAW and DI plants
Management guided H2 FY26 to FY27 for Saudi plants. These are strategic anchors — both the margin premium and the strategic value of being a local Saudi supplier are significant. Watch for any slippage in the commissioning timeline across Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls.
EBITDA margin sustaining above 14%
Margin has expanded from 8.2% (FY23) to 14.7% (9M FY26). Sustaining above 14% through the capex cycle would confirm the high-value US and Saudi mix is structurally improving profitability. A drop below 12% for two consecutive quarters would indicate input cost pressure or margin dilution from lower-value segments.

Verdict

Where are we in the story?

The Investment Case in 175 Words

Welspun Corp is a late-middle-chapters story. The structural tailwinds — US LNG and data-centre pipeline buildout, Saudi Vision 2030, India JJM revival — are not speculative; they are funded and underway. The quality signal is unambiguous: ROCE has gone from 7.9% in FY23 to 24.4% in 9M FY26, EBITDA margins have nearly doubled, and the company sits in a net cash position despite deploying ₹1,722 crore in capex in nine months. The near-term catalyst is the commissioning of new US LSAW and Saudi mills in FY27 — these will add high-margin capacity at a time when the global order book is a record ₹25,350 crore. At 18–19x trailing earnings, the stock is cheap relative to pipeline peers who trade at 35–50x — the discount reflects execution risk on simultaneous projects. That risk is real, but management's track record of hitting 1 million tonnes globally for 8 of 10 years suggests this team knows how to execute. This is a company mid-transition from Indian pipe maker to global infrastructure manufacturer — and that repricing is still incomplete.
