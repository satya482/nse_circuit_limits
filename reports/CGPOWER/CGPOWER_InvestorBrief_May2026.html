The Big Picture

India's First-Ever Multi-Vector Power Capex Upcycle

India is in the midst of something its power sector has never seen before — a simultaneous capex surge across thermal generation, renewables, transmission, and distribution. Citi Research forecasts a 5–6% CAGR for the power sector through 2030, with GENCO capacity additions between FY26 and FY32 expected to be 2.3–2.5 times the levels of FY19–FY25. That is not incremental growth — it is a structural step change.

The India electrical equipment market, valued at roughly USD 44 billion in 2024, is projected to grow at a CAGR of 10.8–15.6% through 2029, adding an estimated USD 95 billion in new demand. The Union Budget FY27 allocated ₹12.2 lakh crore in total capital expenditure (up 12% YoY), with ₹29,997 crore for the Ministry of Power alone (up 39%). The Reform-Linked Distribution Sector Scheme saw its allocation jump from ₹156.7 billion to ₹180 billion.

Layered on top of this are two global tailwinds: a severe transformer shortage (delivery times stretching to 3–5 years worldwide) and explosive data centre demand, with the global data centre transformer market crossing USD 10 billion in 2026. Indian manufacturers with scale, quality certifications, and available capacity are suddenly the most sought-after suppliers in the world.

CG Power sits right at the intersection of all these tailwinds — domestic power capex, export opportunity, and semiconductor ambition. That is why NOW is the time to understand this company.

How It Works

From Turnaround to Flywheel

CG Power makes the heavy electrical equipment that keeps India's lights on and its factories running. The company operates through two core business segments — Power Systems (transformers, switchgear, circuit breakers for power transmission and distribution) and Industrial Systems (motors, drives, railway traction equipment) — plus a newly launched third leg: Semiconductors, through subsidiary CG Semi.

The revenue flywheel works like this: India builds more generation capacity, which requires more transmission and distribution infrastructure, and CG Power supplies the transformers and switchgear. That installed base creates a recurring relationship for upgrades, spares, and additional orders. Meanwhile, every new factory, data centre, and railway line needs motors and drives from the Industrial side.

Customer stickiness comes from three sources. First, power equipment requires stringent testing, type-approval certifications, and long qualification cycles — once a utility qualifies CG Power's 765 kV transformers, switching costs are enormous. Second, the Murugappa Group brand (promoters with 56.4% stake) carries deep institutional trust with government utilities and PSUs like Power Grid Corporation. Third, the company's pan-India manufacturing footprint and service network means it can deliver and maintain equipment where competitors cannot reach easily.

The balance sheet tells the turnaround story better than anything: from a negative net worth of minus ₹2,081 crore in FY20, the company is now virtually debt-free with ₹41 crore in borrowings and a net-debt-to-equity ratio of negative 0.17. The Murugappa acquisition in 2020 was a corporate rescue that has turned into one of the decade's great value-creation stories.

Export and Data Centres

The US and Global Opportunity — The Margin Multiplier

The global transformer shortage has turned CG Power from a purely domestic player into an export story. The ₹900 crore Tallgrass order for US data centre transformers is the company's largest-ever single export win. Management is targeting 35–40% of new capacity for exports. With US transformer delivery times at 3–5 years and more than half of planned data centres reportedly facing delays due to transformer shortages, CG Power's ability to deliver on competitive timelines is a genuine competitive advantage.

Semiconductors — CG Semi

India's OSAT Moonshot — The Long-Term Bet

CG Semi, a joint venture with Japan's Renesas Electronics and Thailand's Stars Microelectronics, launched one of India's first end-to-end OSAT (Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test) facilities in Sanand, Gujarat. The G1 facility offers 0.5 million units per day capacity and targets commercial production in 2026. The G2 facility (14.5 million units per day) is under construction and expected by end-2026. Total investment: ₹7,600 crore (approximately USD 870 million) over five years, backed by central and state government incentives. FY26 consolidated loss: ₹108 crore — expected at this stage of a semiconductor ramp.

Solar and Wind Transformers — The Policy-Driven Tailwind

India's renewable capacity additions require dedicated grid-integration transformers and switchgear. CG Power supplies transformers specifically designed for solar and wind farm interconnection. As India pushes toward its 500 GW renewable energy target, every gigawatt of new solar or wind capacity needs dedicated step-up transformers — and CG Power is a qualified supplier for both domestic utilities and international renewable developers.

The One Thing

What the Market Underappreciates

Strategic Insight

The market sees CG Power as a transformer company that happens to be doing well. What it misses is the optionality structure. The core electrical business (ROCE 37.5%, virtually debt-free, order book at 1.4 times revenue) is a cash-generating machine that is now funding a ₹7,600 crore semiconductor bet without taking on meaningful debt. CG Semi is not a distraction — it is what you get when a company with 37% ROCE decides to self-fund a generational capital allocation move.

The ₹3,000 crore QIP further strengthened this position, nearly doubling equity. If CG Semi works, this company transforms from a capital goods cyclical into a diversified industrial-plus-semiconductor platform — a profile that commands a completely different valuation multiple in markets like Korea, Taiwan, or Japan. If CG Semi does not work, the core business is strong enough to absorb the losses without existential risk. It is a convex payoff structure — limited downside from the bet, massive upside if it plays out.

Eyes Open

What Could Go Wrong

⚠️
Valuation Compression Risk
At 109–117x PE and 17x book, CG Power is priced for perfection. Any earnings miss, margin squeeze, or slowdown in order intake could trigger a sharp de-rating. The stock has already seen a 31% correction from its 52-week high, demonstrating this vulnerability. A reversion to even ABB-like multiples (80x) would imply meaningful downside.
⚠️
CRGO Steel and Copper Volatility
Transformers are materials-intensive — CRGO steel and copper are the primary inputs. A sharp spike in either commodity can compress margins faster than CG Power can pass through costs, especially on fixed-price contracts in the order backlog. OPM has already fluctuated between 11–15% quarterly, showing margin sensitivity.
⚠️
Semiconductor Execution Risk
CG Semi is a ₹7,600 crore bet on a business the company has never operated before. OSAT is scale-driven and technology-cycle-sensitive. FY26 consolidated loss was ₹108 crore, and the G2 facility must scale from construction to 14.5 million units per day — a massive operational ramp. If technology cycles shift or partnerships fray, losses could widen before they narrow.
⚠️
Export Concentration and Tariff Risk
Management is targeting 35–40% of new capacity for exports, with the US as a key market. A single ₹900 crore US order represents significant concentration. Changes in US trade policy, tariff regimes, or Buy America provisions could suddenly close off the most lucrative export opportunity.
⚠️
Railway Segment Operational Fragility
Service issues and supply stoppages in the railway traction business hurt margins earlier in FY26, requiring a leadership change. While management says the segment is stabilising, the episode revealed that operational excellence in specialised government-facing segments requires constant vigilance. Indian Railways contracts carry penalty clauses and reputational consequences.

What to Track

Five Metrics Every Quarter

Order Intake Growth (Quarterly)
Good: Sustained above 25% YoY (Q4 FY26 was 39%). Red flag: Growth falling below 15% or order book declining QoQ — would signal demand peaking or market share loss.
Power Systems PBIT Margin
Good: Sustaining above 20% (Q4 FY26: 23.8%). Red flag: Falling below 18% — would suggest cost pass-through is failing or competitive pricing pressure is intensifying.
CG Semi Revenue and Loss Trajectory
Good: First commercial revenue by Q2 FY27 and losses narrowing QoQ. Red flag: G2 delays beyond CY2026, losses widening past ₹150 Cr per year, or partnership friction signals.
Export Order Mix and Delivery Execution
Good: Export share of order book trending toward 20%+ with on-time delivery. Red flag: Major export orders seeing delays, penalty clauses triggered, or new US tariff announcements impacting Indian transformer imports.
Working Capital Cycle (Cash Conversion Days)
Good: Cash conversion cycle below 40 days (FY25: 35 days) and CFO/OP above 80%. Red flag: Debtor days crossing 80+ or cash conversion cycle breaching 50 days — would signal execution strain from the rapidly growing order book.
Verdict

CG Power is riding three structural tailwinds simultaneously — India's unprecedented power capex cycle (₹9.15 lakh crore planned in transmission alone), a global transformer shortage that has turned Indian manufacturers into export champions, and the country's semiconductor sovereignty push through CG Semi. The financial quality signals are strong: 37.5% ROCE, virtually zero debt, a ₹17,107 crore order book growing at 61% YoY, and CFO consistently converting at 85%+ of operating profit. The near-term re-rating catalyst is sustained export order wins and the CG Semi G1 commercial ramp — both expected in the next 2–3 quarters. At approximately 110x PE, the valuation is undeniably stretched and prices in near-flawless execution; peers like ABB (80x) and Siemens (62x) offer similar sector exposure at lower multiples, though without the semiconductor optionality. We are in the middle chapters of this story — the turnaround is complete, the growth flywheel is spinning, but the semiconductor bet and export ramp are still early innings. The convexity is in CG Semi: if it works, the company re-rates as an industrial-semiconductor platform; if it does not, the core business remains robust enough to justify a capital goods premium — just not this one.
