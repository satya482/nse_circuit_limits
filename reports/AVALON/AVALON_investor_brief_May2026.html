The big picture

Why electronics manufacturing — and why now

India's electronics sector has gone from a cottage industry to a geopolitical storyline in under a decade. Smartphones are now India's second-largest export in 2025-26, up from seventh in 2022. The shift isn't accidental — it's PLI schemes, tariff walls around China, and a global supply-chain reordering that nobody is unwinding.

The India EMS sector grew at a 27% CAGR over FY24–29E (Macquarie, Frost & Sullivan), against a global EMS CAGR of around 5–6%. Indian EMS firms had roughly 4–5% global market share as of 2024. That number has nowhere to go but up. Government tailwinds are substantial: Budget 2026 enhanced the PLI outlay for large-scale electronics manufacturing to ₹40,000 Cr, and MEITY's Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme approved ₹41,863 Cr in fresh investments in January 2026 alone, targeting production of ₹2.58 lakh crore.

Avalon sits in the high-value end of this — not mobile phones for mass market, but electronics for railways, aerospace, clean energy, and industrial automation. These sectors have long product lifecycles, zero tolerance for failure, and switching costs that make churn almost impossible once you're designed in. That's a different business from assembling consumer electronics.

Sector signal: India's electronics exports grew 9× from FY15 to FY25 — a 24% CAGR — and advanced from 7th to 2nd largest export category. The China+1 rebalancing is still in early innings, with India holding only ~4–5% of global EMS share.

The business

What Avalon actually does — and why it sticks

Avalon started in Chennai in 1999 as a printed circuit board assembler. Over 25 years it added layer after layer: cable harnesses, magnetics, sheet metal fabrication, injection-molded plastics, and finally complete box-build solutions. Today it delivers an entire electronic system, not just a component. Box-build revenue was 54% of FY26 sales — meaning more than half its revenue comes from delivering an integrated product, not subcontracting a slice of one.

The flywheel works like this: Avalon gets involved at the design stage of a customer's product. Its engineers co-develop the electronics architecture, sign NDAs, and become embedded in the product's bill of materials. From that point, switching EMS vendors means re-qualifying components, re-certifying assemblies, and rebuilding relationships — a process that takes 18–24 months and costs real money. Customers simply don't do it. Key clients include Collins Aerospace, Meggitt, and Kyosan India — names that suggest this isn't low-margin commodity manufacturing.

The second structural advantage is the dual-shore model. Avalon has manufacturing in Chennai (India) and in the United States via its subsidiary, ABV Electronics (doing business as Sienna Corporation). This isn't just geographic diversification — it's a deliberate customer acquisition engine, especially relevant in the current tariff environment. When a US customer faces import uncertainty, Avalon can manufacture locally. Once tariffs stabilize, production transitions to the more cost-efficient India facility. The US facility is the front door; India is the profit engine.

Revenue verticals

Five growth stories, each with its own catalyst

Avalon's revenue is spread across five distinct verticals, each with an independent external driver. This is not a single-thesis bet — it's five different tailwinds blowing at once, at different stages of maturity.

Industrial & Power Live

Automation, data centres, power electronics

Largest vertical at ~34% of revenue. Includes industrial automation systems, power conversion, and semiconductor equipment manufacturing for a major global partner (undisclosed). Semiconductor equipment prototype completed H1 FY26; full production targeted FY27.

Revenue share: ~34% · ISM 2.0 orders active

Mobility & Aerospace Live

US defence, aerospace cabin sub-assemblies, automotive

~27% of revenue. Collins Aerospace and Meggitt are anchor customers. US facility handles aerospace cabin subassemblies and defence-grade electronics. India's defence localisation push under Atmanirbhar Bharat adds domestic opportunity layered on top.

Revenue share: ~27% · Long-cycle, sticky programs

Clean Energy & EV Ramping

US energy storage boom + India drone motors (Zepco JV)

~20% of revenue. Management guided 60–70% growth in US energy storage demand. The Zepco collaboration handles drone motor design for India. As US IRA incentives phase out, Avalon's plan is to migrate manufacturing to the lower-cost India base — using the US facility to win and India to execute.

Revenue share: ~20% · IRA phase-out is risk + migration opportunity

Railways (Kavach) Pre-prod

₹1.3 trillion FY27 railway safety budget · Kavach mandate

The next big revenue unlock. Kavach — India's indigenous automatic train protection system — has completed testing. Commercial production expected H2 FY27. India plans Kavach rollout across 40,000 km of high-density routes by 2030–32. Only 1,465 km equipped so far: barely 2% of the target. The market potential is ₹45,000–50,000 Cr.

Revenue contribution: FY27 onwards · ₹1.3T safety budget FY27

Communications Ramping

5G radio rollout across India and global markets

Smallest but fastest-growing vertical — posted ~102% YoY growth in Q1 FY26 and is maintaining momentum. Focus is on 5G base station radios. Not a dominant revenue contributor yet, but the growth rate signals this is early innings for a long cycle.

Revenue share: ~7% · 5G radios, 100%+ YoY growth Q1 FY26

The non-obvious insight

The US subsidiary is the customer acquisition engine, not the profit centre

Here is the thing most investors miss when they look at Avalon's consolidated numbers: Sienna Corporation (ABV Electronics, USA) contributed approximately ₹758 Cr in FY26 revenue — nearly half the consolidated total. India's standalone revenue was ₹821 Cr. On a pure revenue basis, this is almost equally an American EMS company as an Indian one.

But the economics are not symmetric. India's manufacturing EBITDA margin was running at 16.7% in Q3 FY26 (9M FY26: 14.8%). The US operation carries higher costs — labour, compliance, overheads — and serves primarily to win relationships with US customers and manage tariff exposure. Once customers are locked in and tariffs are manageable, Avalon transitions production to Chennai, where margins are structurally better.

This model has a beautiful logic: the US factory is the front door for Boeing-ecosystem customers who won't source from a pure India-based vendor. India is where the margin is made. The implication for investors is that as the India-to-US mix gradually tilts back toward India over FY27–29 (as clean energy and industrial programs migrate), consolidated margins should structurally expand — without needing to win a single new customer.

The single thing to remember: Avalon's US subsidiary is a customer acquisition tool, not the profit centre. India manufacturing at 16–17% EBITDA drives the economics; the US facility at lower margins wins the customers. As US programs gradually migrate to India, margin expansion is almost mechanical.

Eyes open

Five real risks — not disclaimers

⚠
Sienna concentration — the US growth engine is also the biggest single-point risk
ABV Electronics (Sienna) contributed ~₹758 Cr of FY26 consolidated revenue — nearly 47% of the total. India standalone grew 30%, but Sienna drove the bulk of incremental growth. Any US demand softness — whether from end-market slowdown, IRA policy reversal, or tariff escalation in reverse — hits consolidated revenue hard. The "Indian EMS story" is actually deeply US-dependent right now.
Red flag signal: US business growth rate dropping below 25% for two consecutive quarters.
⚠
Working capital intensity — ₹463 Cr in inventory, only ₹71 Cr cash
EMS is inherently capital-intensive in working capital (inventory + receivables). At 112 days, Avalon's cycle is improving but still heavy. ₹463 Cr inventory versus ₹71 Cr cash means the company is funding its supply chain on credit. Any demand disruption or customer payment delay can tighten liquidity quickly. The Kavach and semiconductor ramps will require additional inventory build before revenues materialise.
Red flag signal: Working capital days reversing above 130 days or trade payable stretching above 90 days.
⚠
Kavach timeline risk — commercial production is not yet confirmed
The Kavach programme has been "expected in H2 FY27" for several quarters. India's national train protection system rollout has a history of delays — as of late FY26, only 1,465 km of the targeted 40,000 km network was equipped. Government programme execution risk is real. If Kavach commercial orders slip to FY28, a meaningful revenue line expected by the market simply isn't there.
Red flag signal: No Kavach purchase order announcement by Q2 FY27.
⚠
IRA policy reversal — US clean energy incentives under political pressure
Management itself guided "minimal impact from the Beautiful Bill" on clean energy, but acknowledged the risk. US energy storage demand has been driving Sienna's growth at 60–70% CAGR. If IRA-linked incentives erode — as political winds in Washington have suggested — demand for US-assembled energy storage systems could slow materially before the India manufacturing migration can absorb the volumes.
Red flag signal: Clean energy revenue growth dropping below 20% for two consecutive quarters.
⚠
Component import dependence — India still imports 88% of PCBs
India's EMS sector remains structurally reliant on imported components. China and Hong Kong account for 54% of electronics component imports (FY25). Any tariff escalation, supply disruption, or yuan depreciation can squeeze gross margins by 100+ basis points — as seen in prior quarters. Avalon's 33–35% gross margin guidance is defensible in normal conditions, but a component supply shock could compress it rapidly.
Red flag signal: Gross margin dipping below 31% for any quarter.

Verdict · May 2026

Avalon Technologies is a genuine structural play on three converging themes: India's electronics manufacturing buildout (27% sector CAGR with policy tailwinds through 2029), US-to-India manufacturing migration (the Sienna model converting US customers into India-manufactured volumes over time), and two emerging verticals in Kavach railways and semiconductor equipment that haven't contributed a rupee to FY26 revenue yet. The quality signal in the financials is directional — seven consecutive quarters of sequential improvement, PAT growing 78% on 46% revenue growth, and working capital tightening. ROCE at 12.8% is mediocre for the multiple being paid, but India-manufacturing-only ROCE (implied by the 16–17% India EBITDA) is far more attractive and should expand as the mix tilts. The near-term catalyst is a Kavach commercial production announcement in H2 FY27 — a single announcement that could re-rate the stock as a confirmed "railway electronics" play. On valuation, at ~78× trailing P/E versus peers at 54–70×, Avalon is pricing in near-perfect execution: Kavach, semiconductor, and clean energy migration all hitting on schedule. That's a lot of simultaneous moving parts. We are in the middle chapters of this story — the heavy lifting of establishing the model is done, but the biggest revenue catalysts (Kavach, semiconductor, US-to-India migration) are still over the horizon. For investors who believe in the structural thesis and management's execution track record, the price is demanding but the addressable opportunity is genuinely large. For those who need near-term earnings certainty to justify the multiple, the wait for Kavach confirmation is the right approach.
