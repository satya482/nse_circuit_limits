Context

Why Thermax, and why now

India’s industrial capex cycle is in full swing, and the BSE Capital Goods index hit an all-time high in the same week Thermax reported its best quarterly order intake ever. Three structural forces are converging at Thermax’s doorstep simultaneously.

Power capacity revival

India is building new thermal plants using ultra-supercritical (USC) technology — cleaner, more efficient, and requiring specialized boiler engineering that very few companies can deliver. The global industrial boiler market stands at USD 12.1 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at a CAGR of 5.4% through 2035 to reach USD 20.7 billion. Thermax ranks among the top five players globally by market share.

Industrial decarbonization mandate

Budget 2026 allocated Rs 200 billion over five years for carbon capture technologies across power, steel, cement, and chemicals. The SIGHT scheme (Rs 4,440 crore, FY26 to FY30) incentivizes domestic electrolyser manufacturing. India’s National Green Hydrogen Mission targets 5 million metric tonnes per year of production capacity by 2030, backed by Rs 19,744 crore in total government commitment.

India industrial manufacturing surge

Rising demand from metal, cement, food and beverage, refinery, and chemical sectors — all of which need Thermax’s heat, cooling, water treatment, and pollution control solutions. International demand is growing too: the Dangote Nigeria refinery order (Rs 580 crore) is a visible example of how Thermax’s brand travels.
2
Business model

Engineering solutions across the industrial energy stack

Thermax does two things in parallel: it sells products (small boilers, chillers, water treatment systems, chemicals) that generate recurring, shorter-cycle revenue; and it executes large infrastructure projects (power plants, industrial utility systems, EPC contracts) that are lumpy but high-value. The combination gives it both base revenue stability and upside from capital expenditure cycles.

What makes customers sticky: once a Thermax boiler or water treatment plant is commissioned at a facility, the company typically handles service and maintenance for years. Large EPC projects require proprietary technology, code compliance, and supply chain relationships that cannot easily be replicated by a new entrant. The relationship with Dangote Industries — spanning multiple decades and multiple contracts across refineries — is a template for how deep these ties run.

The flywheel

Equipment sale leads to a service contract, which deepens into digital energy management via the ExactSpace acquisition (Thermax now holds 51%), which creates data that justifies expanded scope, which generates the next order. This loop compounds over time and makes each customer relationship stickier than the last.
3
Vertical stories

Four businesses, each with a distinct external driver

Industrial Products Running

Approximately 40 percent of revenue. Small capacity boilers, absorption chillers, air pollution control systems, water and waste recycle, EPC services. Serves metal, cement, food and beverage sectors. Revenue grew 55 percent between FY22 and FY24 on India’s manufacturing surge. Steady compounder with service revenue stickiness.

Industrial Infra (TBWES) Ramping

Largest segment by revenue. Large boilers, power plant EPC, fired heaters, waste heat recovery. TBWES (joint venture with Babcock and Wilcox) is the only credible domestic alternative to BHEL for ultra-supercritical boilers. Landmark Rs 1,600 crore order in Q4 FY26 for 800 MW USC plant is one of the largest in company history. Rs 580 crore Dangote Nigeria order is the international proof-point.

Green Solutions Early stage

Renewable energy, bio-CNG, green hydrogen, solar captive power, decarbonization consulting. HydrogenPro exclusive partnership (August 2025) gives India-exclusive rights to alkaline electrolyser supply and EPC. SIGHT scheme creates direct demand pull. First wind-solar hybrid captive power plant commissioned in Gujarat. R&D underway in carbon capture, coal gasification (with IIT Delhi), and waste-to-aviation fuel.

Chemicals Headwinds

Ion exchange resins, performance chemicals, construction chemicals including admixtures, waterproofing, and flooring. Revenue broadly flat in FY26. Profit before interest and tax dropped from Rs 30 crore to Rs 16 crore due to higher input costs and product mix shift. No clear near-term catalyst; margin stabilization is the watch item.

4
Strategic insight

The boiler is the bridge technology

Most investors see Thermax as a company caught between coal (old economy) and green hydrogen (new economy). The insight is the opposite: Thermax’s ultra-supercritical boiler expertise is the exact same engineering knowledge-base that underpins industrial decarbonization — heat exchange at extreme pressures and temperatures, combustion chemistry, process integration for complex fuels.

The TBWES capability that won the Rs 1,600 crore USC coal boiler order is the same capability that makes Thermax credible in carbon capture heat exchangers, green hydrogen balance-of-plant systems, and waste-to-energy boilers. This is not a pivot; it is a translation of the same core skill into tomorrow’s demand. No greenfield startup can replicate 60 years of high-pressure boiler engineering in five years.

Risks — eyes open

Five specific things that could go wrong

  • △

    Working capital squeeze on large project execution

    Operating cash flow halved to Rs 541 crore in FY26 from Rs 1,080 crore — a direct consequence of milestone-based cash collection on large EPC projects. The Rs 1,600 crore USC boiler contract will intensify this further. Red flag: OCF remaining below Rs 600 crore for two consecutive quarters in FY27.

  • △

    Cost inflation eroding project margins

    Raw materials rose 7.68 percent year on year in Q4, employee costs 17.9 percent, and interest costs 35.5 percent. Large fixed-price EPC contracts signed today may face margin compression if commodity prices spike during the 24 to 36 month execution window. Signal: EBITDA margin falling below 9 percent.

  • △

    Large project revenue lumpiness

    The Rs 1,600 crore TBWES boiler contract will contribute revenue only when project milestones are met. If commissioning delays occur due to customer readiness, site access, or regulatory approvals, revenue recognition could slip by quarters. Do not be alarmed by a weak Q1 FY27 alone.

  • △

    Green hydrogen optionality may be overpriced in the stock

    India’s green hydrogen market is still in early commercial stage — most announced projects are under feasibility or pilot phase. The HydrogenPro partnership is a positioning move, not yet an order pipeline. If green hydrogen scaling is delayed 3 to 5 years (likely), the high PE multiple has no near-term justification from this segment. Monitor actual electrolyser orders received, not memoranda of understanding signed.

  • △

    Chemicals segment margin deterioration

    Profit before interest and tax dropped from Rs 30 crore to Rs 16 crore on a flat revenue base — margins nearly halved. If chemicals PBIT does not recover to Rs 25 crore or above by H2 FY27, it signals structural issues rather than a cyclical blip.

7
Watch signals

Five metrics to check every quarter

  • Order intake quarterly run-rate

    Sustained Rs 3,000 crore or above per quarter is bullish and confirms the Q4 FY26 surge was not a one-off. Below Rs 2,500 crore for two consecutive quarters signals pipeline thinning. Watch for the next large-project anchor order in FY27.

  • EBITDA margin: target 10 percent or above

    Q4 FY26 margin of 10.92 percent was the highest in recent quarters. Can this be sustained as the TBWES boiler contract ramps (which may carry tighter margins due to technology transfer costs)? Below 9 percent for a full quarter is a margin-compression signal.

  • Operating cash flow recovery

    OCF should recover as large project milestones are billed and collected. Rs 700 crore or above in H1 FY27 is a healthy sign. Continuing below Rs 500 crore means working capital deterioration is structural, not just a project timing issue.

  • Green Solutions: first confirmed electrolyser order

    Any confirmed purchase order (not a memorandum of understanding) for green hydrogen production using HydrogenPro’s alkaline electrolyser technology would be a genuine re-rating catalyst. Size above Rs 50 crore equals meaningful. Also watch for bio-CNG plant commissioning as a nearer-term proof-point.

  • Chemicals PBIT stabilization

    Chemicals PBIT needs to recover to Rs 25 crore or above by H2 FY27. If it continues declining below Rs 12 crore, the segment may need a strategic review. Watch for any announcement around product mix, price hikes, or capacity rationalization.

Verdict

Thermax enters FY27 riding three structural tailwinds: India’s thermal power capacity addition cycle, rising industrial capex demand across metal, cement, and refining, and the early innings of industrial decarbonization — all of which play directly to its unique boiler and process engineering capability. The quality signal in the financials is mixed but leaning positive: ROCE of 18.8 percent, a five-year EBIT CAGR of 36 percent, and a record Rs 13,604 crore order book all argue for a quality compounder; however, the halving of operating cash flow to Rs 541 crore in FY26 warrants close monitoring as large projects ramp. The near-term re-rating catalyst is clean execution of the Rs 1,600 crore ultra-supercritical boiler contract with visible margin delivery by Q2 or Q3 FY27; beyond that, the first confirmed green hydrogen order could open an entirely new valuation conversation. At approximately 75x TTM PE — in line with Thermax’s own 3-year average — the stock is not cheap, but it is not unusually stretched either. This is a middle-chapter story: the structural thesis is established, the order book is fattening, but the most exciting chapter — green energy revenue — has not been written yet.
