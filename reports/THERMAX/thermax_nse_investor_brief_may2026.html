Context Why Thermax, and why now

India's industrial capex cycle is in full swing, and the BSE Capital Goods index hit an all-time high in the same week Thermax reported its best quarterly order intake ever. Three structural forces are converging at Thermax's doorstep simultaneously.

Power capacity revival: India is building new thermal plants using ultra-supercritical (USC) technology — cleaner, more efficient, and requiring specialized boiler engineering that very few companies can deliver. The global industrial boiler market is USD 12.1B and growing at 5.4% CAGR to 2035; Thermax ranks among the top 5 globally.
Industrial decarbonization mandate: Budget 2026 allocated ₹200 billion over 5 years for carbon capture technologies across power, steel, cement, and chemicals. The SIGHT scheme (₹4,440 Cr, FY26–FY30) incentivizes domestic electrolyser manufacturing. India's National Green Hydrogen Mission targets 5 MMT/year capacity by 2030, backed by ₹19,744 Cr in total government commitment.
India industrial manufacturing surge: Rising demand from metal, cement, food & beverage, refinery and chemical sectors — all of which need Thermax's heat, cooling, water treatment, and pollution control solutions. International demand is growing too: the Dangote Nigeria refinery order (₹580 Cr) is a visible example.
2
Business model Engineering solutions across the industrial energy stack

Thermax does two things in parallel: it sells products (small boilers, chillers, water treatment systems, chemicals) that generate recurring, shorter-cycle revenue; and it executes large infrastructure projects (power plants, industrial utility systems, EPC contracts) that are lumpy but high-value. The combination gives it both base revenue stability and upside from capital expenditure cycles.

What makes customers sticky: once a Thermax boiler or water treatment plant is commissioned at a facility, the company typically handles the service and maintenance relationship for years. Large EPC projects (like ultra-supercritical power plants) require proprietary technology, code compliance, and supply chain relationships that can't easily be replicated by a new entrant. The relationship with Dangote Industries — spanning multiple decades and multiple contracts across refineries — is a template for how deep these ties run.

The flywheel: Equipment sale → service contract → digital energy management → expanded scope → new order. The recent ExactSpace acquisition (raised Thermax holding to 51%) extends this loop into digital energy intelligence, adding a software layer to the hardware relationship.

Ion exchange resins, performance chemicals, construction chemicals (admixtures, waterproofing, flooring). Revenue broadly flat in FY26. PBIT dropped from ₹30 Cr to ₹16 Cr due to higher input costs and product mix shift. No clear near-term catalyst; margin stabilization is the watch item.
4
Strategic insight The boiler is the bridge technology
Most investors see Thermax as a company caught between coal (old economy) and green hydrogen (new economy). The insight is the opposite: Thermax's ultra-supercritical boiler expertise is the exact same engineering knowledge-base that underpins industrial decarbonization — heat exchange at extreme pressures and temperatures, combustion chemistry, process integration for complex fuels. The TBWES capability that won the ₹1,600 Cr USC coal boiler order is the same capability that makes Thermax credible in carbon capture heat exchangers, green hydrogen balance-of-plant systems, and waste-to-energy boilers. This isn't a pivot; it's a translation of the same core skill into tomorrow's demand. No greenfield startup can replicate 60 years of high-pressure boiler engineering in five years.

Risks — eyes open Five specific things that could go wrong
Working capital squeeze on large project execution
Operating cash flow halved to ₹541 Cr in FY26 from ₹1,080 Cr — a direct consequence of milestone-based cash collection on large EPC projects. The ₹1,600 Cr USC boiler contract will intensify this further. Red flag: OCF remaining below ₹600 Cr for two consecutive quarters in FY27.
Cost inflation eroding project margins
Raw materials cost rose 7.68% YoY in Q4, employee costs 17.9% YoY, and interest cost 35.5% YoY. Large fixed-price EPC contracts signed at today's material costs may face margin compression if commodity prices spike during the 24–36 month execution window. Signal: EBITDA margin falling below 9%.
Large project revenue lumpiness
The ₹1,600 Cr TBWES boiler contract will contribute revenue only when project milestones are met — quarterly revenue and margins will be uneven. If commissioning delays occur (customer readiness, site access, regulatory approvals), revenue recognition could slip quarters. Don't be alarmed by a weak Q1 FY27.
Green hydrogen optionality may be overpriced in the stock
India's green hydrogen market is still in early commercial stage — most announced projects are under feasibility or pilot phase. The HydrogenPro partnership is a positioning move, not yet an order pipeline. If green hydrogen scaling is delayed 3–5 years (likely), the high PE multiple has no near-term justification from this segment. Monitor: actual electrolyser orders received, not MoUs signed.
Chemicals segment margin deterioration
PBIT dropped from ₹30 Cr to ₹16 Cr on a flat revenue base — margins nearly halved. This is a low-visibility drag that compounds over time. If chemicals segment PBIT does not recover to ₹25+ Cr by H2 FY27, it signals structural issues (competition, input cost pass-through failure) rather than a cyclical blip.
7
Watch signals Five metrics to check every quarter
Order intake quarterly run-rate
Sustained ₹3,000+ Cr per quarter is bullish and confirms the Q4 FY26 surge was not a one-off. Below ₹2,500 Cr for two consecutive quarters signals pipeline thinning. The ₹1,600 Cr USC boiler order is once-in-a-cycle; watch for the next large-project anchor order in FY27.
EBITDA margin: target 10%+
Q4 FY26 margin of 10.92% was the highest in recent quarters. Can this be sustained as the TBWES boiler contract ramps (which may have tighter margins due to technology transfer costs)? Below 9% for a full quarter is a margin-compression signal.
Operating cash flow recovery
OCF should recover as large project milestones are billed and collected. OCF of ₹700 Cr or above in H1 FY27 is a healthy sign. Continuing below ₹500 Cr means working capital deterioration is structural, not just a project timing issue.
Green Solutions: first real electrolyser order
Any confirmed purchase order (not MoU) for green hydrogen production using HydrogenPro's alkaline electrolyser technology would be a genuine re-rating catalyst. Size above ₹50 Cr = meaningful. Also watch for bio-CNG plant commissioning as a nearer-term proof point.
Chemicals PBIT stabilization
Chemicals PBIT needs to recover to ₹25+ Cr by H2 FY27. If it continues declining (below ₹12 Cr), the segment may need a strategic review. Watch for any announcement around product mix, price hikes, or capacity rationalization.
Verdict
Thermax enters FY27 riding three structural tailwinds — India's thermal power capacity addition cycle, rising industrial capex demand across metal, cement, and refining, and the early innings of industrial decarbonization — all of which play directly to its unique boiler and process engineering capability. The quality signal in the financials is mixed but leaning positive: ROCE of 18.8%, a 5-year EBIT CAGR of 36%, and a record ₹13,604 Cr order book all argue for a quality compounder; however, the halving of operating cash flow to ₹541 Cr in FY26 warrants monitoring as large projects ramp. The near-term re-rating catalyst is clean execution of the ₹1,600 Cr ultra-supercritical boiler contract with visible margin delivery by Q2–Q3 FY27; beyond that, the first confirmed green hydrogen order could open an entirely new valuation conversation. At ~75x TTM PE — in line with Thermax's own 3-year average — the stock is not cheap, but it is not unusually stretched either. This is a middle-chapter story: the structural thesis is established, the book is fattening, but the most exciting chapter (green energy revenue) hasn't been written yet.
