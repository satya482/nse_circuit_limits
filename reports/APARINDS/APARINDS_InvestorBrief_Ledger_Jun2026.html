Macro Context
The Grid Supercycle — And Why APAR Is at Its Centre
India has committed ₹9.15 lakh crore (₹9.15 trillion) to rebuilding its power transmission grid by 2032 — a spend that dwarfs any prior infrastructure programme in Indian electricity history. Every transmission line built in India requires aluminium conductors. APAR makes more of them than any other company on the planet.

India's renewable ambitions are not merely aspirational — they are creating a physical infrastructure bottleneck. With 258 GW of renewable capacity installed as of December 2025, and a target of 500 GW by 2030, the country needs to move massive amounts of clean power from solar-rich deserts and wind-swept coasts to power-hungry cities. This requires transmission lines, and transmission lines require conductors. Power Grid Corporation of India (PGCIL) alone has planned capital expenditure of over ₹1.08 lakh crore across FY26–FY28 — ₹28,000 crore in FY26 growing to ₹45,000 crore by FY28, making this the single fastest ramp of transmission investment in India's electricity history.

The sector context extends beyond India. Globally, the transmission and distribution market was valued at $330 billion in 2023 and is projected to grow at 3.9% CAGR through 2030, with Asia-Pacific accounting for the largest single regional spend. India's domestic cables market — currently around $21 billion in FY25 — is growing at approximately 9% CAGR driven by data centre expansion, 5G rollout, railway electrification, and industrial demand. India's highest-ever annual renewable capacity addition in 2025 — nearly 38 GW of solar and 6.3 GW of wind — means grid infrastructure spending is not a future promise but a present reality demanding APAR's products today.

The timing is additionally compelling because substation additions grew 55% YoY in April–November 2025, even as transmission line additions lagged plan by about 15%, signalling a catch-up in physical execution that should drive conductor order books upward through FY27 and FY28.

India's ₹9.15-trillion T&D mandate is the largest conductor demand upcycle in the country's history; APAR's position at the top of the global conductor food chain is structural, not cyclical.

Business Model
Three Businesses, One Grid — A Complementary Flywheel
APAR does not make one grid product — it makes three distinct products that are all required by the same customer (an electricity utility or industrial developer) at different points in the grid's lifecycle. This is the simplest version of why APAR is structurally stickier than it looks from the revenue mix alone.

In plain English: APAR makes the wires that transmit power (conductors), the oil that insulates transformers (specialty oils), and the cables that distribute power from substations to end-users (cables). These three products serve a single buyer — the electricity infrastructure ecosystem — with different switching costs, margin profiles, and growth trajectories.

The business model has a natural flywheel: conductor volumes (high, commodity-linked) generate consistent EBITDA that funds the higher-capex cable manufacturing buildout, which in turn earns higher-margin contracts from data centre, solar farm, and industrial customers who cannot tolerate delivery failure. The specialty oils business, meanwhile, provides counter-cyclical stability — transformer oil demand grows whenever transformers are installed, which happens regardless of whether the grid is being built for renewables or demand growth.

What makes customers sticky? In conductors, each utility has its own technical specifications and material standards — once APAR is qualified to supply PGCIL or a state utility, competitors face a 12–24 month re-qualification process. In specialty oils, APAR's 400+ product grades and position as a dominant domestic supplier creates logistics stickiness that purely import-reliant alternatives cannot match. In cables, complex project specifications and the growing requirement for domestic manufacturing content under public procurement rules further entrench APAR's position.

Three products, one buyer ecosystem: conductors generate cash, oils provide annuity, cables are the margin re-rating vehicle — each reinforces the others' moat.

In specialty oils, APAR's 350-grade product portfolio and UAE manufacturing base create further barriers. Threat of Substitutes Low For conductors, High-Temperature Low-Sag (HTLS) conductors are the primary substitute for conventional ACSR — and APAR already manufactures them, turning the substitution risk into a premiumisation opportunity. The 45.8% premium product mix in FY26 shows APAR is capturing the substitution rather than being displaced by it. For transformer oils, the long-term risk is the EV transition reducing transformer demand, but India's grid build cycle ensures 10+ years of structural demand. Underground cables are a partial substitute for overhead conductors in urban settings, and APAR makes these too.
Structural Synthesis

Supplier power (commodity input price volatility with imperfect pass-through) and buyer power (tender-based utility procurement at scale) are the two forces most structurally constraining APAR's EBITDA margin — creating the 8.5–10% band that management has so far been unable to durably exceed. The threat of new entrants and substitutes are well-managed by APAR's product range and relationships. Rivalry is manageable domestically but intensifying in export markets from Chinese competition.

Commodity inputs and utility tender discipline create a structural 9% margin ceiling; breaking to 10–11% requires premium cable mix to reach 30%+ of revenue.

Vertical Stories
Three Businesses at Three Different Points in Their Growth Arc
⚡ CONDUCTORS — Running at Full Speed
The World #1 That Still Has Runway

The conductor business is APAR's engine room — and in FY26 it fired on every cylinder. Revenue grew 32.7% to ₹12,712 Cr, volume reached 241,788 metric tonnes (+8.6%), and crucially, EBITDA per tonne climbed to ₹43,012 from ₹36,683 — a 17.3% improvement that reflects APAR's deliberate shift toward premium conductors. Premium products (HTLS, AL-59 high-efficiency types) now account for 45.8% of conductor volume, up from 40.6% a year ago. This is not just higher revenue per tonne — it is fundamentally more defensible business because premium conductors require utility-specific engineering and technical approval that commodity ACSR does not.

The US market has been APAR's most exciting conductor export story — US revenue grew 83% YoY in Q1 FY26 and 55.2% for the full FY26 year, before the Section 232 tariff shock. The underlying US demand for Indian conductors is structural: America needs to upgrade an ageing grid, Indian suppliers offer quality and competitive pricing, and APAR is the most certified Indian exporter. Section 232 created a pause, but not a reversal. New order inflows in FY26 reached ₹11,450 Cr (+24.2% YoY), and the pending order book of ₹7,671 Cr (38.9% export) gives 9+ months of forward visibility.

External catalyst: PGCIL's ₹1.08 lakh crore FY26-28 capex + NEP 105,000 circuit-km line addition target by 2032
🛢 SPECIALTY OILS — The Annuity Engine
The Quiet Compounder With a Global Footprint

The transformer oil business is APAR's least flashy division but arguably its most strategically valuable franchise. With roughly 45% domestic market share, 60% share specifically in power transformer oil (the high-voltage segment), and the ability to supply over 350 grades to 120+ countries, this is a business built on depth that competitors cannot replicate quickly. A utility planning a transformer for a 765 kV substation cannot switch transformer oil suppliers mid-project — the oil specification is built into the transformer's insulation design, making APAR effectively a mandated supplier for the transformer's lifetime.

FY26 volumes grew 9% to 631,985 KL, with transformer oil volume up 10.6% in Q3. However, EBITDA per KL slipped to ₹5,942 from ₹6,145 — reflecting softer realisations rather than demand weakness. The division's strategic expansion includes a new storage terminal at JNPT (Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust) — a ₹200 Cr investment that dramatically improves bulk export logistics for Middle East, Africa, and Southeast Asian markets where APAR has an existing UAE manufacturing subsidiary (APAR Petroleum Specialties FZE). Automotive and industrial lubricant sub-segments also growing at 14-16% YoY.

External catalyst: India transformer manufacturing growing to accommodate 595,000 MVA new substation capacity (NEP target); global transformer oil market tied to power infrastructure spending
🔌 CABLES — The Margin Re-Rating Vehicle
From ₹6,220 Cr to ₹10,000 Cr: The Make-or-Break Bet

The cable business is where APAR's future valuation premium will be won or lost. FY26 revenue reached ₹6,220 Cr (+25.8% YoY), making cables the company's fastest-growing division as a contributor to total revenue. India's cable market, at ~$21 Bn in FY25 growing at 9% CAGR, is itself attractive. But APAR's ambition is not just to grow with the market — it is to premiumise into specialty and renewable cables where margins approach 10% and customers are stickier (data centres, solar farms, and EV charging infrastructure do not switch cable suppliers mid-project).

Management has guided ₹10,000 Cr cable revenue by FY29, backed by an ₹800 Cr investment in a new cable manufacturing facility that management says will unlock ₹10,000 Cr of revenue-generating capacity. The new CEO, Chandrashekhar Shrotri (formerly Segment CEO at Siemens for India, ASEAN & Africa), brings exactly the kind of global execution muscle needed to capture export contracts from data centre developers and utility-scale solar investors. A pending order book of ₹1,900 Cr provides visible near-term revenue.

External catalyst: India cable market ~$21 Bn growing 9% CAGR; data centre capex surge; 5G rollout; EV charging infrastructure; US grid modernisation (subject to tariff resolution)
Conductors are running; oils are compounding; cables are the bet — the FY29 ₹10,000 Cr cable target requires tripling a business that took 20 years to reach ₹6,000 Cr.

Strategic Insight
The Conductor Crown Funds the Cable Coronation
🎯 THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND ABOUT APAR

Most investors value APAR as a conductor company with a cable division. That framing is structurally wrong. APAR is a cable company using conductor profits as venture capital.

The conductor business — world's #1 by volume, generating ₹1,040 Cr of EBITDA in FY26 — is not the long-term prize. It is the funding mechanism. Every ₹100 of conductor EBITDA that APAR generates subsidises the ₹800 Cr cable factory being built on a 66-acre greenfield plot. Every quarter of strong conductor cash flow is a quarter where APAR does not need to dilute equity or take on meaningful debt to fund its cable ambitions. The balance sheet — D/E of 0.10x — is not caution; it is optionality preserved deliberately so that the cable business can be built on APAR's terms, not a banker's.

Why does this matter for valuation? Because cable companies command 10-12% EBITDA margins vs. conductor companies at 7-8%. As cables reach 30-35% of APAR's revenue (vs 27% now), blended margins structurally re-rate. The market is already pricing some of this — APAR at 52x PE is not a conductor multiple (KEC International trades at 20x). But whether APAR earns its current valuation depends entirely on executing the cable buildout on time and at margin. The conductor crown is beautiful. The cable factory is the destination.

There is a second non-obvious insight embedded in APAR's transformer oil position. The company commands 45% of India's domestic market for transformer oil. This is not just revenue — it is information. Because APAR supplies transformer oil to utilities and OEMs across every major power project, APAR's oil salesforce effectively sees India's grid investment pipeline 12–18 months before it becomes public news. This market intelligence feeds directly into conductor and cable order positioning, giving APAR a systematic lead-time advantage that competitors without an oil business cannot replicate.

APAR's conductor dominance is the flywheel; cable premiumisation is the destination; transformer oil market intelligence is the unfair information advantage few investors recognise.

Risk Register
Five Specific Risks — With Their Measurable Signals
1. US Section 232 Tariff Escalation — The Export Cliff Risk
Electrical conductors now face a 25% Section 232 tariff on full customs value in the US market (April 2026 proclamation). Electrical grid equipment has a temporary 15% rate through 2027. In Q3 FY26, this collapsed US conductor exports by 65.4% YoY, wiping ₹400+ Cr of quarterly revenue in a single quarter. The risk is that the 15% temporary rate on grid equipment becomes 25% post-2027, or that a Section 301 action targets Indian cables specifically. Given APAR's 55% FY26 US revenue growth, any permanent tariff escalation would be material — potentially reducing FY27 cable exports by ₹500–800 Cr versus the unhedged scenario.
📡 Watch signal: Any US trade action announcement on electrical equipment from India; quarterly US revenue as % of APAR's cable segment revenue.
2. Commodity Price Spike — Aluminium and Copper Margin Compression
Aluminium is ~60-70% of conductor manufacturing cost; copper is a primary input for cables. Both are globally traded metals with no single-source alternatives and imperfect pass-through to utility customers. A sharp aluminium price spike (as occurred in 2021-22) can compress EBITDA margins by 200-300 bps in a single quarter even with pass-through contracts, because of timing lags between input cost recognition and customer billing. This is the structural constraint identified in the Porter's analysis — and it is why APAR's blended EBITDA margin has been stuck at 9% despite 23%+ revenue growth.
📡 Watch signal: LME Aluminium price (quarterly average); gross margin vs EBITDA margin spread — widening suggests cost absorption.
3. Cable Execution Risk — From ₹6,220 Cr to ₹10,000 Cr in Three Years
Management has committed to growing cable revenue from ₹6,220 Cr in FY26 to ₹10,000 Cr by FY29 — a 60% increase in three years. This requires: (a) the ₹800 Cr cable factory to commission on time and ramp to full utilisation, (b) the new CEO Chandrashekhar Shrotri to win new export contracts, (c) the domestic premium cable market to absorb incremental capacity without margin dilution. The cable CEO resignation in December 2025 (mid-execution) is a governance yellow flag. The pending cable order book of ₹1,900 Cr is only 8-9 months of current revenue run-rate — not yet a confident forward ledger for the FY29 target.
📡 Watch signal: Cable segment order book (should reach ₹3,000+ Cr if the FY29 target is real); cable EBITDA margin (should reach 10%+ vs current ~9%).
4. Domestic T&D Capex Slowdown — PGCIL Right-of-Way Delays
Domestic transmission line additions in April–December 2025 were 15% below plan, partly because of Right-of-Way (RoW) delays and transformer bushing shortages that are delaying substation work. If these execution bottlenecks persist — as they have intermittently for the past three years — PGCIL's ₹45,000 Cr FY28 capex plan may deliver less physical output than guided. Conductor demand is directly linked to circuit-kilometres of line commissioned, not capex sanctioned. A 15-20% slippage in PGCIL and state utility line additions could reduce conductor volume growth to 5-6% vs the 10-12% management expects.
📡 Watch signal: Monthly Ministry of Power T&D additions data (available from CEA); PGCIL quarterly capitalisation rate vs capex incurred.
5. Chinese Conductor Competition in Export Markets
In Q3 FY26, conductor export volumes de-grew 5.9% YoY, specifically attributed by management to "intensified competition from non-US geographies." Chinese conductor manufacturers — operating with state subsidies and lower cost structures — are aggressively pricing into Middle East, Southeast Asia, and African markets where APAR has historically been the dominant Indian exporter. If Chinese competition structurally compresses export realisations, the premium conductor EBITDA/MT improvement narrative (which drove the stock's re-rating) could reverse — particularly if APAR's export mix shifts from US (high-value) toward China-contested markets (price-sensitive).
📡 Watch signal: Conductor export mix by region (US vs Middle East vs Africa); EBITDA/MT trend — any reversal from ₹43,000+ suggests pricing pressure.
The US tariff risk is the most binary — it already caused one severe quarterly miss; cable execution risk is the most consequential for long-term thesis; Chinese competition is the most underappreciated structural threat.

Watch Signals
Five Metrics to Check Every Quarter — With Red Flag Thresholds
1. Conductor EBITDA per Metric Tonne (Capex Watch)
What it is: The single most revealing conductor segment metric — it combines volume, premium product mix, and realisation quality in one number. Currently ₹43,012/MT (FY26), vs management's medium-term guidance baseline of ₹35,000–36,000/MT as "floor." Green flag: ₹40,000+ with growing premium mix share. Red flag: Sub-₹35,000 for two consecutive quarters with flat/declining premium mix — signals pricing pressure or volume dumping at margin cost.
2. Cable Segment Revenue Run-Rate vs ₹10,000 Cr FY29 Target (Margin Watch)
What it is: Current cable revenue is ₹6,220 Cr (FY26). To reach ₹10,000 Cr by FY29, APAR needs to add ~₹1,260 Cr/year over three years — roughly 20%+ CAGR. Track quarterly cable revenue YoY and sequential growth. Green flag: Cable growing at 22%+ with order book above ₹3,000 Cr. Red flag: Two consecutive quarters of cable growth below 15% — implies FY29 target needs revision, likely triggering PE de-rating toward Polycab range.
3. EBITDA Margin — Can the 9% Ceiling Break to 10%? (Margin Watch)
What it is: APAR's blended EBITDA margin has been essentially flat at 9.0% for two years despite 23%+ revenue growth. Management has guided 10–11% as the aspiration, tied to cable premiumisation. Green flag: EBITDA margin reaching and sustaining 9.5%+ for two consecutive quarters — signals the cable mix is beginning to flow through. Red flag: Any quarter below 8.0% — below the structural 5-year average — which would signal input cost shock or margin dilution from new capacity.
4. FY27 Capex Deployment Rate — Is the ₹1,500 Cr Planned Spend Actually Happening? (Capex Watch)
What it is: Management guided ₹1,500 Cr capex for FY27, up from ₹740 Cr actual in FY26. This is a Ledger-active flag — capex was slow-paced in FY26 (57% of plan). Check cash flow from investing activities quarterly. Green flag: ₹375 Cr+ capex per quarter on a trailing basis (annualising to ₹1,500 Cr). Red flag: Q1/Q2 FY27 capex below ₹300 Cr per quarter — implies completion drift and pushes cable factory commissioning toward FY28, making FY29 ₹10,000 Cr target essentially impossible.
5. US Market Recovery — Conductor and Cable Export Revenue Trajectory (Capital Watch)
What it is: The US was APAR's fastest-growing export market in H1 FY26 (+83% conductors, +130% cables) before Section 232 caused Q3 collapse (-65% US exports). The April 2026 tariff proclamation introduces a temporary 15% rate on grid equipment through 2027. Track US revenue as a % of total exports and compare to prior H1. Green flag: US revenue exceeding ₹400 Cr/quarter by Q2 FY27 — signals tariff adaptation (pass-through pricing to US buyers working). Red flag: US revenue below ₹200 Cr for three consecutive quarters — implies tariff is structural, not temporary, and the export bull case needs fundamental revision.
EBITDA/MT on conductors and cable revenue run-rate are the two most important quarterly numbers; capex deployment rate is the leading indicator for both targets being met.
📌 INVESTMENT VERDICT — MID-CHAPTER COMPOUNDER

APAR Industries sits at the intersection of three structural tailwinds: India's ₹9.15 lakh crore T&D capex supercycle (which directly feeds conductor and cable demand), global grid infrastructure investment (which drives export opportunities), and India's renewable integration mandate (which is creating new-category demand for specialty cables to solar farms, EV infrastructure, and data centres). The quality signals are genuinely strong — ROCE of ~34%, D/E of 0.10x, zero promoter pledge, and CFO that turned positive in FY25 after being negative in FY24. These are not marginal improvements; they represent a company with genuine financial health and self-funding capacity.

The near-term catalyst that could re-rate the stock is a clean FY27 Q1 result showing: cable revenue above ₹1,800 Cr (+25%+ YoY), capex deployment at ₹350+ Cr for the quarter, and EBITDA margin at or above 9.2%. Any two of three would likely trigger analyst upgrades. On valuation: at 52x PE, APAR is priced at a slight premium to cable peers (Polycab at 45x, KEI at 48x) — the premium reflects the conductor global position and US export option, but the market is pricing the cable ₹10,000 Cr target as a base case. We are in the middle chapters of this story — the conductor thesis is proven, the cable thesis is in execution. The next 24 months will determine whether APAR re-rates further toward a pure cable multiple or corrects toward the conductor multiple.
