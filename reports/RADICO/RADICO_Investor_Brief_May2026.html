01
Context
India's Drinking Upgrade Cycle — And Why Now

India is the world's most interesting spirits market right now, and not because of volume — it's because of the direction of spend. The country's total alcohol market is a USD 208 billion industry in 2026, but the value action is concentrated in the upper tier. The premium spirits segment alone is forecast to grow at a 12.2% CAGR from 2026 to 2033, reaching USD 26.8 billion — more than doubling in seven years, driven by urban income growth, a young demographic, and rising social acceptance of fine drinking.

The structural driver is simple: India has roughly 350 million working-age adults in cities, a rising professional class, and per-capita spirits consumption that is still a fraction of developed markets. The aspirational arc from mass whisky to craft gin to aged single malt is happening faster than anyone projected. Whisky alone holds 60%+ of the domestic market share by volume; vodka is underpenetrated at under 5% of IMFL volumes against 25–30% globally — an enormous whitespace waiting to be captured.

Karnataka — India's second-largest IMFL state, contributing roughly 15% of national volumes — announced a landmark policy reform in March 2026: abolishing state-controlled retail price fixation and introducing Alcohol-in-Beverage (AIB) based taxation from April 2026. This is the most significant alcobev regulatory liberalisation in India in two decades, and it directly benefits premium-branded players like Radico who compete on quality, not cheapest-per-unit. Pricing flexibility, historically a constraint, is now a competitive weapon.

Meanwhile, the Indian craft spirits category — Rampur's natural habitat — is on its own exponential trajectory. IMARC projects the India craft spirits market to reach USD 20.2 billion by 2034, a 21.87% CAGR. This is the global "Indian single malt" moment: the world is discovering that Himalayan aging in extreme seasonal temperatures produces whiskies that mature faster and with greater complexity than Scottish equivalents.

Why Now

The confluence of rising domestic premiumisation, regulatory liberalisation in key states, and global recognition of Indian single malts has arrived simultaneously — creating a rare multi-year growth window for the incumbents who built infrastructure before the demand inflected.

India's premium spirits market is on a 12.2% CAGR curve to 2033; regulatory reform in Karnataka adds near-term pricing power for branded players.
02
Business Model
The Mass-to-Luxury Flywheel

Radico Khaitan is one of India's oldest and largest IMFL companies, founded in 1943 as Rampur Distillery Company. It makes and sells spirits — whisky, vodka, rum, gin, brandy — across every price tier from ₹200 to ₹20,000 a bottle. But the business model is more elegant than a diversified spirits shelf suggests.

Think of Radico as a three-tier pyramid. At the base are the mass brands: 8PM Whisky, Contessa Rum, Old Admiral Brandy — millionaire brands (selling over 1 million cases per year each) that generate volume, cashflow, and production scale. They are not glamorous, but they are the engine room. The mass brands fund the working capital, the distillery investment, and the marketing budgets that build the premium brands above — making Radico's mass portfolio the silent financier of its luxury ambitions.

In the middle tier sits the Prestige and Above (P&A) range — Magic Moments Vodka (8.6 million cases, 60% market share in Indian vodka), After Dark Whisky (3.1 million+ cases), 8PM Premium Black, Royal Ranthambore Whisky — brands growing at 20–50%+ per year and driving realization upgrades. This is where volume meets value.

At the top is the luxury layer: Rampur Indian Single Malt (Himalayan distilled, globally distributed), Jaisalmer Indian Craft Gin, Sangam World Malt, and the newest entrant Spirit of Kashmyr Luxury Vodka. These brands don't scale in cases — they scale in value and brand equity.

Revenue Flywheel

Mass volume → operating leverage → margin improvement → marketing budget for premium brands → premium brand success → average realisation upgrades → higher EBITDA on the same case volume. The loop is self-reinforcing.

Backward Integration Moat

Radico owns ENA (Extra Neutral Alcohol) capacity at Rampur and Sitapur totalling 321 million litres — enough for 6–7 years of growth visibility. Competitors who buy ENA externally face input cost volatility that Radico does not.

Customer stickiness is structural: 60–65% of bottling is handled through lease arrangements with third-party bottling partners across states (essential given India's state-by-state distribution regime), while 30–35% is in-house. The CSD (Canteen Stores Department, Ministry of Defence) channel provides a particularly captive, barrier-to-entry-protected revenue stream — Radico is one of the largest IMFL suppliers to India's armed forces, with a 26–27% share.

Mass brands are not the problem — they are the capital engine that funds Radico's luxury pyramid; the flywheel is now turning visibly in the financials.

Threat of New Entrants Low Three compounding barriers: (1) CSD registration requires years of track record; (2) state-by-state label registration and excise compliance is a 2–5 year process per state; (3) ENA distillery capex (₹750 Cr for Sitapur plant) is prohibitive for challengers. Craft distillery startups exist at the micro level but cannot achieve the volume to challenge Radico's economics.
Threat of Substitutes Medium Beer and wine compete at the entry end; RTD (ready-to-drink) cocktails are a nascent category growing in urban India. Imported Scotch competes in the super-premium tier — but import duties maintain a significant price disadvantage for imports versus domestically produced premium spirits like Rampur. The risk is episodic import duty reductions, not structural.
Structural Pressure Synthesis

The two forces that matter most: (1) State government buyer power — as intermediary price-setters, state excise departments remain the most consequential single actor in Radico's economics; policy changes in Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra in FY26 directly impacted regular volumes. (2) Rivalry intensity — United Spirits' Diageo parentage means access to global supply chains, advertising muscle, and import brand portfolio that Radico must outmanoeuvre through domestic authenticity and CSD moat.

Structural moats are strong — the state excise intermediary layer is the primary swing factor between a good and great year for Radico.

Strategic Insight
The One Thing to Understand

Most investors look at Radico and see a mid-sized spirits company with a lot of brands across a lot of segments — complex, hard to pin down. The insight they miss is architectural.

Radico's mass brands are not a legacy problem to be solved — they are the deliberate funding mechanism for India's most ambitious luxury spirits globalisation play. Every case of 8PM Whisky sold at ₹250 effectively subsidises a bottle of Rampur Single Malt making its way onto a Michelin-starred bar's menu in Dubai.

This is unusual in spirits globally. Diageo acquired its way up the ladder (buying Johnnie Walker, Talisker, etc). Pernod Ricard is a portfolio of separate acquisitions. Radico grew both ends organically from one distillery complex in Rampur, Uttar Pradesh — meaning the cost structure is integrated in a way that is extremely hard to replicate.

The strategic implication: Radico's EBITDA margin expansion (from ~12% trough in FY23 to 16.8% in FY26 and guided 18%+ by FY27) is not a cyclical recovery — it is operating leverage from the pyramid filling in at the top. Every luxury rupee of revenue that Rampur earns carries incremental margins significantly above the corporate average, so the mix shift alone is a mathematically guaranteed margin expansion engine for the next 3–5 years as the luxury portfolio scales from ₹475 Cr today to what management has internally described as a multi-thousand-crore business.

"This recognition reflects the strong growth trajectory of Rampur and our broader international portfolio, as we continue to build relevance with the global bar community." — Sanjeev Banga, President International Business, Radico Khaitan, January 2026

The second non-obvious thing: Radico's CSD moat is more valuable than the balance sheet suggests. Government procurement relationships in India take 5–10 years to build, require track record, and cannot be fast-followed. CSD is a recurring, recession-resistant, price-stable channel representing hundreds of thousands of defence personnel with reliable income. Competitors who benchmark Radico only against consumer brand metrics are missing this.

Radico's mass brands are the silent engine of its luxury globalisation — not a drag but the structural moat that no challenger can replicate without 80 years of distillery heritage.

Risks — Eyes Open
What Could Derail the Story
⚠️
State Excise Policy Disruption
The most material and recurring risk. Radico's regular volumes in Q4 FY26 were dented by policy changes in Maharashtra and Karnataka, and the route-to-market shift in Andhra Pradesh required a multi-quarter adjustment. Excise taxes can represent 60–80% of final retail price in some states — a single adverse state policy can wipe an entire quarter's regular volume growth overnight, with zero prior warning. Signal to watch: any adverse change in state excise policy in UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, or Andhra Pradesh.
⚠️
Valuation-to-Execution Gap
At 78x trailing PE, even a one-quarter miss on EBITDA margin or luxury portfolio growth could compress the multiple significantly. The market has priced in near-flawless execution through FY27 and FY28. If management's guided +125 bps EBITDA margin expansion lands at 60–70 bps instead, the stock could de-rate 20–30% without any deterioration in the underlying business. This is priced-to-perfection territory.
⚠️
Raw Material Volatility — Grain and ENA
Broken rice and maize are the primary inputs for grain-based ENA. In FY22–24, grain price inflation caused 500 bps of gross margin compression. The FCI surplus-driven softening in FY26 has helped, but this can reverse quickly with monsoon failure or policy changes. Radico's Sitapur ownership provides supply security but not price immunity — it still pays market prices for grain. Glass costs are secondary but also volatile.
⚠️
Luxury Brand Scale Timing Risk
The investment thesis depends significantly on the luxury portfolio (currently ₹475 Cr) continuing to compound at 25%+ annually. Rampur's global traction is real (Drinks International #4 ranking, Michelin Guide partnership) but global spirits is a market where brand building takes 8–12 years and a single bad batch or brand misstep can reverse years of premium equity. If D'YAVOL tequila misfires or Rampur extensions fail to resonate, the margin expansion story loses its primary driver.
⚠️
Competitive Intensification from Diageo / Pernod Ricard
United Spirits (Diageo JV) has a ₹99,100 Cr market cap and access to Diageo's global brands, marketing dollars, and supply chain. If Diageo decides to aggressively compete in Radico's prestige segments (whisky at ₹1,000–5,000 price band), the marketing spend required to defend share could compress Radico's A&P-to-sales ratio discipline (currently managed at 6–8% of IMFL revenues). Pernod Ricard India remains unlisted but is a fierce operator in the premium end.
State excise policy is the primary uncontrollable risk — everything else Radico can manage; adverse state-level decisions cannot be hedged.

Watch Signals
Five Metrics to Track Every Quarter
1. Luxury Portfolio Quarterly Revenue Run-Rate
Track the rolling 4-quarter total luxury sales value. FY26 full-year was ₹475 Cr — so target run-rate is ₹119 Cr/quarter for flat, and ₹148 Cr/quarter for management's 25% FY27 growth. Green flag: any quarter above ₹140 Cr. Red flag: sequential decline in luxury revenue, which would suggest global bar demand or domestic premium demand is softening.
2. EBITDA Margin Trajectory
Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin was 19% — a record. FY27 guidance is full-year 125 bps expansion (implying ~18.1% for the year). Track if Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin holds above 16% (seasonally weaker quarter). Green flag: margin above 17% in H1 FY27. Red flag: any guidance revision downward citing grain prices or state-specific volume losses.
3. Prestige & Above Volume Growth vs. Guidance
Management guided 20% P&A volume growth in FY27 from the 16.70 million case base (target: ~20 million cases). Track quarterly P&A volume YoY growth — this is the core indicator of whether premiumisation is sustaining. Green flag: consecutive quarters of 20%+ P&A volume growth. Red flag: two consecutive quarters below 15% growth would suggest the premiumisation cycle is peaking.
4. Net Debt / Debt-Free Milestone
Radico is guiding debt-free status by H1 FY27. Net debt was already reduced by ₹329 Cr in FY26. At Q1 FY27 concall, verify whether they are on track. A debt-free balance sheet will trigger a formal dividend payout ramp (min 20% of PAT), which could bring new income-oriented institutional investors into the register. Green flag: confirmation of zero net debt by September 2026. Red flag: any new capex announcement above ₹200 Cr that pushes out the debt-free date.
5. D'YAVOL Tequila Launch Execution
Radico has guided a D'YAVOL Añejo tequila launch in end-FY27 (Q3–Q4 FY27), priced ₹20,000–30,000. Watch for: (1) actual launch date vs. guidance; (2) state approvals (excise clearance state-by-state is a multi-month process); (3) initial sell-in data if available at the FY27 year-end call. A delayed launch is a minor negative; a launch with strong on-trade adoption (bar menus in top Michelin-tier restaurants) would be a significant positive signal for the luxury portfolio mix.
If luxury portfolio quarterly run-rate and EBITDA margin both sustain above guidance, the re-rating thesis holds; watch for either of these softening before the financials do.
💡 Verdict — Mid-Chapter Story
Radico Khaitan sits at the intersection of three structural tailwinds that are unlikely to reverse: India's demographic-driven premiumisation wave, global appetite for authentic "origin-story" spirits (Indian single malt's moment is now), and regulatory liberalisation in key states creating pricing freedom that was unavailable five years ago. The quality signal in the financials is unambiguous — EBITDA margins near-doubled from a 12% trough to 16.8% in three years, PAT grew 75.9% in FY26, and the company is on track to be debt-free in H1 FY27, unlocking consistent dividend payouts. The near-term catalyst is the luxury portfolio sustaining its 25% growth guidance in FY27, which would bring EBITDA margins above 18% and validate the market's premium multiple. At 78x trailing PE, the stock is not cheap — it is priced as a secular compounder, and it will require near-flawless execution on its luxury and premium pillars to justify that premium over industry peers like United Spirits trading at 55–60x. The story is mid-chapter: the thesis is proven, the investment cycle is complete, and the FCF harvest is beginning — but the market has already paid for the next two chapters, leaving investors requiring patience and vigilance on execution rather than conviction on direction.
